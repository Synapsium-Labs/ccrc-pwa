## What the measurement found

Measured on `971287c72`; each item names its census (all in C5d), so a later task reads the same number.

1. **The refusal helpers (ARCH-45).** `ccd/ccd#_reap_refuse` and `ccd/ccd#_ws_collect_refuse` have byte-identical bodies (`REAP_VERDICT="$1"; REAP_DETAIL="$2"; return 1;`) and print nothing. `_reap_refuse` has 114 call occurrences in 19 functions (`_ws_reap_eval` 52, `_ws_reclaim_ladder` 29, the rest in `_ws_reclaim_*`, `_ws_expire_*` and `_ws_reap_leaf_ok`), 113 with a literal word and one, `_reap_refuse "${row#!}"`, re-emitting a word python chose; one call is in `ccd/lib/disk-retention.sh` (the stale arm). `_ws_collect_refuse` has 41, all literal, in 10 functions (`_ws_collect_*` and `_ws_loose_*`), 12 of them in the library's loose arm. **The two families are separated exactly by region:** every `_ws_collect_refuse` call lies inside the `COLLECT-BEGIN`/`COLLECT-END` region (29) or the `LOOSE-BEGIN`/`LOOSE-END` region (12), and every `_reap_refuse` call lies outside both (114). `archived-expiry-policy.test.ts` already reads those two regions by those markers. Census: `refuseCalls()`.
2. **The refusal token stays where it is.** `refusal-v1` is printed by `ccd/ccd#_lc_refuse` (the anchored machine line before its die, #560), never by either helper. The spec's exit "with `refusal-v1` in the one helper" is superseded by the ruling (D-7073).
3. **The scanners.** Fifteen test files read the two names. A naive rename (measured on a scratch copy, then discarded) turns **13 tests in 10 files** red: `archived-expiry-policy` (2), `ccd-cwd-users` (1), `ccd-reclaim-main-identity` (1), `ccd-refusal-scan` (1), `ccd-wsaudit-nonpoison` (1), `child-reclaim` (1), `collect-pacing` (1), `collect-readers` (2), `workspace-refusal-census` (1), `wsaudit` (2). **Five more stay green and go blind:** `ccd-actor-flags` (a floor of 30 that other shapes still clear), `ccd-swap-refuse` and `ccd-collect-audit` (a "no line in a harvested shape" check whose shape no longer occurs anywhere), `ccd-wsaudit-nonpoison`'s shape row (the same), and `ccd-refusal-record` and `ccd-child-reclaim-pause` (the name only in a message or a comment). A blind guard is re-pointed exactly as a red one is. And a merged name over the whole file would merge the two token sets: `wsaudit.test.ts` would then need a sentence for every collector word (`quarantine-kept`, `witness-mismatch`, `not-loose`, …), and `ccd-collect-audit.test.ts`'s `WS-AUDIT-COLLECT` block, which holds 29 collector calls and 0 reap calls, would fail its "no harvested shape" row. So every harvest is scoped by family (C5e).
4. **The token sets each scanner builds today** (the pins C5d lands), by the scanner's own regex over its own slice: the reap family over the whole source, 49 words (`wsaudit`, `ccd-swap-refuse`, `ccd-actor-flags`); the reap family in `_ws_reap_eval`, 35 (`workspace-refusal-census`); in the `RECLAIM` region less comments, 14 (`child-reclaim`); in the `EXPIRE` region less comments, 12 (`archived-expiry-policy`, before it adds `_ws_reclaim_ladder`'s); the collector family over the whole source, 10 (`collect-pacing`); in the `COLLECT` region, 9 (`ccd-refusal-scan`); and `ccd-wsaudit-nonpoison`'s combined four-shape set outside the reclaim region, 55. C5d writes each as a literal sorted list, measured, not copied from this sentence.
5. **Token plumbing.** Two functions hash a token: `ccd/ccd#_ws_fingerprint` (reap's fourteen positional facts, written as `name=value` lines) and `ccd/ccd#_ws_reclaim_fingerprint` (`printf '%s\n' "$@" | _plat_sha256 | cut -d' ' -f1` over named inputs; reclaim, expire, collect, loose and orphan tokens all use it, and `_ws_collect_token` wraps it). The reap's fourteen lines are exactly what `_ws_reclaim_fingerprint` hashes when handed `"id=$1" … "childrenDigest=${14}"`, so the encoding can be one with no token changing. Census: `tokenMinters()`, 2. The re-proof comparisons (`"$token" != "$REAP_TOKEN"` in `_ws_reap_locked`, `_ws_reclaim_locked`, `_ws_expire_locked`; the record compares in the collector) each answer their own verb's refusal and stay.
6. **Lock plumbing.** Eight functions open `$REG/.reap-$id.lock`. Four of them, `cmd_ws_reap`, `cmd_ws_reclaim`, `cmd_ws_expire` and `cmd_ws_collect`, carry the same wrapper: `command -v flock` or `_lc_refuse <act> flock-unavailable` (one sentence, the same in all four), `exec {lfd}>>` or `_lc_refuse <act> lock-unopenable` (the same sentence), `flock -n` or `_lc_emit <act> refused "$id" "" verb ws-<verb> refusal in-progress detail <sentence>` plus the `{"refused":"in-progress",…}` document at rc 0 (the sentence differs per verb), then the locked function, the close and `return "$rc"`. The other four are different shapes: `cmd_ws_restore` dies on contention (`_lc_refuse restore … in-progress`, its own sentences) and holds the lock for the rest of the verb; `_ws_expire_spawn_gate`, `_ws_collect_audit_contained` and `_ws_collect_registered` probe or name the lock and act under it on nothing. The orphan lock (`.orphan-<s16>.lock`) and the loose lock (`.collect-loose.lock`) are other files. Census: `reapLockWrappers()`, 4, with the four other shapes reported by name.
7. **The tail steps (ARCH-45's third part) are already shared.** The steps the spec names, the attic pin, `_reg_purge`, the tombstone and the journal line, are named helpers on `main` with two or more callers each: `_reg_retire` (the one caller of `_reg_purge`; called by `_ws_reap_tail`, `_ws_reclaim_tail`, `cmd_ws_rm`, `_ws_gc_prune_row` and `cmd_forget`), `_ws_unsupervise` (both tails and five other verbs), `_ws_tombstone` (`_ws_reap_tail`, `_ws_reclaim_locked`, `_ws_expire_locked`), `_ws_tomb_str` and `_ws_tomb_children` (both tails and their locked and resume functions), and `_lc_intent`, `_lc_done` and `_lc_fail`. `_reg_purge` call lines outside a named helper: 0 (its one call is in `_reg_retire`). The attic pins are **not** one step: `_ws_attic_pin` (reap) pins the reflog's commits best-effort and counts what it made, while `_ws_reclaim_attic_extra` and `_ws_reclaim_pin*` (reclaim) fail closed under `_ws_reclaim_contained`. That is an opposite safety direction, a catalogue reason, so they stay two. The exit is met, and C5d pins it (D-7074).
8. **ws-reap's stale header line** ("The ONLY destructive verb.") is `ccd/ccd#cmd_ws_reap`'s first comment line; a test file's doc comment repeats it (`server/test/ccd-ws-reap.test.ts`). C5f removes both.
9. **The docs redactor (ARCH-67).** The docs helper (`ccd/ccd#_docs_py`, one python process per docs verb, on fd 3) carries `REDACT_RULES` inside its `docs-parity` block, a four-rule copy of `shared/docs.ts#DOCS_REDACT_RULES` held equal row by row by `docs-parity.test.ts`, and a `redact(s)` function called from five sites, all on failure paths (two stderr heads, `failure_line`'s `detail` and `stderrHead`, `helper_failed_line`'s `detail`), each **before** a cut, so a cut can never split a secret. So "call node once per docs verb" cannot be a single pass over the answer after the python writes it: the cut would come first. The CLI is called from inside the helper, at most once per redacted string, on a failure answer only. No ccd verb runs a `shared/*.mjs` file today; `ccd/ccd-usage-sweep` reads `shared/models.mjs` from the deployed tree (`${CCRC_TREE:-$HOME/ccrc}`), which is the one precedent. The front's python probe (`_docs_front`) answers `helper-unavailable` with `python-missing` or `python-too-old`, which `ccd/ccd`'s doctor code words. Census: `pythonRedactorTables()`, 1.
10. **The log-line redactors (ARCH-67).** Two bash functions: `ccd/ccrc#_upd_redact` (three rules: a scheme's userinfo, the scheme-less userinfo run of D-6748, and `$HOME` to `~` at a path boundary) and `ccd/ccd-update-sync#redact` (two: a scheme's userinfo by extglob, and the same home rule). `ccd/ccd` has none. The spec says they "become one bash function in the platform block both scripts already share byte-for-byte", but the platform block is shared by `ccd/ccd` and `ccd/ccrc` (`macos-platform.test.ts`), and `ccd/ccd-update-sync` does not carry it, so one definition there would still need a copy in `ccd-update-sync`. `ccd/ccrc`'s own comment says the difference is deliberate ("it prints no release base"). Census: `logLineRedactors()`, 2.
11. **`FleetState.ccdVerbs` (ARCH-72).** Three assignment sites: `server/src/remote/client.ts#onReady` (any value, including `null` on an absent or malformed frame: a reset), `server/src/refreshcaps.ts#makeRefreshCaps` (non-null only, and only when the connection epoch is the one it asked under), and the local-mode closure in `server/src/index.ts` (non-null only, no epoch: local mode's epoch stays 0). 12. **Usage sidecars (ARCH-72).** One writer, the statusline (`ccd/statusline-command.sh`, a staged `$$` temp renamed into place; its own temp's cleanup is the writer's). Two deleters: `ccd/ccd#_usage_purge` (bash; its one caller `_reg_purge`; it removes `<id>.json`, the `.<id>.*.tmp` temps and `<id>.agents/`, recording each failure through `_reg_purge_unremoved`) and `ccd/ccd-usage-sweep.py#reap_orphans` (python, run by the `ccd-usage-sweep` timer; it removes `<id>.json` and then `<id>.agents/` only when no `$REG/<id>.uuid` stands and the sidecar's `ts` is readable and more than a day old, and never the temps). ## Review focus (the failure modes most likely to bite)

1. **A token set that moved.** C5e's harvests are scoped by family: the reap family is every helper call outside the `COLLECT` and `LOOSE` regions, the collector family every call inside them. A scanner that now reads the whole file, or a call moved across a region marker, changes a set; C5d's literal lists catch it. Reviewers diff each set at base and tip.
2. **A guard gone blind.** Five of the fifteen scanners stay green under a naive rename (§What the measurement found, item 3). Each must be re-pointed so its mutant still reds: a reap-family word added inside the `WS-AUDIT-COLLECT` block, the swap slice or the lifecycle block must still red its "no harvested shape" row.
3. **`return 1` lost.** `_ws_reap_leaf_ok` ends in `… || _reap_refuse …`, so its status is the helper's (the comment above `_reap_refuse` says so). `_ws_refuse` keeps `return 1`, and the comment moves with it.
4. **A token byte that changed.** C5f hands reap's fourteen facts to `_ws_reclaim_fingerprint` as `name=value` words in today's order. A reordered, renamed or added input changes every reap token: an audit taken before the deploy then reads `state-changed` once at the reap. The pin compares a literal token over fixed facts.
5. **A lock decline that changed.** C5g passes each verb's in-progress sentence verbatim; the flock-unavailable and lock-unopenable sentences are one text today and stay one. The decline answers at rc 0 with the document and the journal row, the locked function runs with the lock held, and the descriptor goes back on every path. `cmd_ws_restore`'s lock is not this wrapper (it dies on contention and holds the lock for the whole verb).
6. **A computed act no scanner can see.** Under C5g the act word reaches `_lc_refuse` and `_lc_emit` as `"$act"`. `ccd-refusal-v1.test.ts`'s "every call site names its token as a literal" counts only `_lc_refuse <literal-act>`, so a call with a computed act is invisible to it today. C5g extends the count to those calls and requires their tokens to be literal.
7. **Redaction after the cut.** C5h keeps every redaction before every `clip_utf8`. A node pass over the finished line would cut first.
8. **A fallback to a stale copy.** With no node or no CLI, the docs verb answers `helper-unavailable`; it never falls back to a python copy of the rules (spec §5.13).
9. **A `null` that now overwrites.** C5j's setter keeps three behaviours apart: a ready frame resets (any value, `null` included); a refresh writes only a non-null answer under the epoch it asked; local mode writes only a non-null answer. One "write if not null" rule for all three would keep a stale list across a reconnect to an older agent.
