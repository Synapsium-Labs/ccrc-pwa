## 2. F1: a long or escaped LF ... CR break at a slice cut (`pane-cut-long-break`)

### 2.1 Measured at `main`

The review's forms, built here (`rowsOf` of `drawer-mask-rows.test.ts`): a 2,000-byte key at 40 columns, 1,800 bytes of
escapes before each row, 200 rows of other text before it, after 1,000 to 50,000 bytes of ordinary lines (8
placements); and an 8,128-byte key at 64 columns, 600 bytes a row (11 placements). Pane history, rows mode,
`maskTextSliced`; "shown" is key positions some 8-byte slice of the key covers, escapes read away (**measured**):

| Separator | 2,000-byte form, `main` | 8,128-byte form, `main` | here |
|---|---|---|---|
| LF, 62 blanks, CR | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| LF, 63 blanks, CR | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| LF, a tab, CR | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| LF, 64, 65 or 66 blanks, CR | 2,000 at 7 of 8, 360 at the other | 8,128 at 10 of 11, 1,371 at the other | 0 |
| LF, 64 NULs, CR | the same | the same | 0 |
| LF, 200 blanks, CR | 2,000 at 8 of 8 | 8,128 at 11 of 11 | 0 |
| LF, SGR, CR | 2,000 at 7 of 8, 360 at the other | 8,128 at 9 of 11, 1,627 and 960 at the others | 0 |
| LF, CUF, CR | the same | the same | 0 |

The key's rows are one chain; a cut that ends it anywhere leaves neither part a whole key, so no fingerprint matches and
the whole key shows. With two rows of 1,000 bytes and 64 to 70,000 blanks, cut at seven offsets around the first slice's
end, `main` and here both show 0 (**measured**): the lookahead's plain overlap (18 KiB) reaches both rows, so only a chain
longer than that overlap needs the grown read this finding is about (**inferred**).

**Reachability** (review 2445, its refuter b, **measured** there): the one pane-history caller captures with
`tmux capture-pane -p -e -J`, and tmux consumes a CR as a cursor move, so the LF ... CR forms cannot arrive through the
real capture. The fix is kept anyway: the masker is the fail-closed boundary, and its readers should agree.

### 2.2 The cause

The slice cut ends a slice at the end of the row break its last LF begins, read within `BREAK_CUT_MAX` (64) bytes of
the LF; past that it cut after the LF, as `main` did before C44. The next slice then began with the blanks and the CR. Its
masking joins rows over the context before it, so it read the break whole; but the slice before reads its lookahead
(`lookaheadEnd`) from the cut, and that read took the blanks and the CR as an empty first row, which ends every key's
rows: "the chain read back from here starts after the cut", no growth, and a key longer than the plain overlap was never
read whole on either side (**measured** with mutant m5, §6). The escape forms failed earlier: the cut read the window as
raw text, where a LF, an escape and a CR is not a `ROW_BREAK`, so it cut after the LF though the break was short.

### 2.3 What was tried first, and why it was not kept (measured)

The review's first proposal: when the break is not proven inside the bound, cut at its start, so the next slice holds the
whole break. Built and measured in a scratch tree: every form leaked, the short and ordinary ones included. Forcing the
cut to every break's start, the 2,000-byte form showed 360 to 2,000 bytes at 4 of 4 placements with LF, two blanks, CR;
with 62 blanks; with CR LF; and with LF alone. The same reader is the cause: a lookahead that begins with a row break
starts with an empty row, so `lookaheadEnd` read every chain as starting after the cut.

### 2.4 The fix (`server/src/accounts/mask.ts`)

- `sliceCutAt`: the cut reads the break through the escape view, as every row reader does, so an escape between the LF
  and the CR is part of it; it ends the slice after the break when two bytes follow it within `BREAK_CUT_MAX` + 2 bytes of
  the LF (what follows a CR decides whether it ends the break, since a CR LF after it does not), else after the LF. The
  window ends outside an escape (`outsideEscape`), so an escape the bound cuts is never read as text.
- `lookaheadEnd`'s `lead`: when the slice was cut after a LF that no CR comes before, the rest of that break at the
  lookahead's start (blanks and escapes, then a CR that no LF or CR LF follows: `BREAK_REST`) is read as part of the
  break: the read's first row starts after it, an all-indent read is measured from there, and the crossing test reads
  `k > lead` for `k > 0`. The slice bound is as at `main`: 65,600 bytes at most (64 KiB plus 64), which C44's F4 cost row pins.

Why it fails closed (**inferred**, then measured on every form): `lead` only moves where the read's first row starts,
from inside a break to after it; a chain that reached the empty row before now reaches the cut, which can only grow the
read (up to its cap, past which the chain's rows are masked, as since C41) and never answer "no cut" where `main`
answered "cut". The view read can only end a slice later, after a whole break, or after the LF as before.

## 3. F3: `BREAK_CUT_MAX` pinned

The value now decides where a slice ends, never what is masked (the F1 row masks 62 to 200 blanks alike). The row "a
slice of pane history ends after a LF, 63 blanks and a CR, and after the LF of 64 blanks or more" puts a break's LF at
65,000 bytes and reads the first slice's own text: 63 blanks end it after the CR (65,065), 64 and 65 after the LF
(65,001), 62 NULs after the CR, an SGR or a CUF between LF and CR after the CR (65,006), a LF alone after the LF. At
`main` it is red on the SGR form (65,001, not 65,006) (**measured**). `BREAK_CUT_MAX` at 63 reds it on 63 blanks, at 65
on 64 blanks (§6).

## 4. F2: `breakStartAt` and `ROW_BREAK`

`server/test/mask-break-grammar.test.ts` (new) runs every string of 1 to 8 bytes over x, LF, CR, space and NUL (488,280
strings, 1,226,729 forward breaks), and for each break `ROW_BREAK` reads forward checks that `breakStartAt` reads back from
its end to its start. They agree everywhere but one family, which the row requires to exist and to be exactly this: a
CR LF (or CR CR LF), blanks or none, then a CR. Forward that is a CR LF, a blank row and a CR; back from the CR,
`breakStartAt` reads LF, blanks, CR as one break that starts at the CR LF's LF (`\r\n\r` back from 3 gives 1, not 2).

Kept, not fixed (**inferred** from the callers, read): a reader that steps back over breaks (the cap's step back in
`lookaheadEnd`) reaches the row before the blank row and reads its run, which can only join more rows to a chain; a
fix there would read the blank row and answer "no cut". The readers that stop at a break's start (`chainBack`,
`rowTailAt`) find the CR LF's CR before the LF either way, which is no run, and stop as they do forward. The
docstring says so. It runs in about 0.2 s (**measured**: the file in 378 ms with vitest's start).

| CR LF, then CUD+CR | 2,000 | 2,000 at 16 of 16 | 8,192 at 16 of 16 | 2,000 at 8 of 8 | 2,000 at 8 of 8 |

### 5.2 The cause and the change

The review inferred `chainBack`. Measured otherwise: on plain rows the whole key shows in one push, which no hold or walk
back decides; the matcher's own row join reads breaks with `ROW_BREAK`, which read CR CR LF as a CR, then a CR LF, an
empty row between, and an empty row ends a key's rows. A terminal draws CR CR LF as CR LF (one row down), which is how a
second output translation (ONLCR twice) writes a newline, so it is the one form here plausible as real output.

`ROW_BREAK` (`server/src/accounts/shapes.ts`, the one grammar) now reads `\r\r?\n` as one break, at most one CR more, so a
run of CRs costs one pass, never a read back per CR; and a LF, blanks, CR is not one break when a CR LF follows the CR, as
it was not when a LF did (so LF, CR CR LF reads as LF then CR CR LF, which `breakStartAt` reads back the same way).
`breakStartAt`'s LF arm reads CR CR LF back; the brute force (§4) covers both. No new mechanism: the change is the one
grammar every reader builds on, so the drawer, pane history, the walk back and the shape rows all read it alike. Fails
closed: rows that were split at an empty row are now joined, which can only mask more (**inferred**; measured 0 on every
CR CR LF form). The row "a key whose rows a CR CR LF breaks" pins it; at `main` it reds on the one-push form (2,000 key
bytes).

### 5.3 What stays open: `drawer-consecutive-breaks`

CR LF CR, CR LF SGR CR, LF LF and CR LF then CUD+CR are two rows down, a blank row between the key's rows, which a
terminal draws as a key with an empty line in it, not a wrap; ending a key's rows at a blank row is the grammar's rule,
and joining across it would mask ordinary text broken by a blank line (**inferred**). CR SGR LF (a colour reset between
the CR and the LF) is one row down that the escape view reads as a CR, a NUL and a LF; it shows up to 2,000 bytes in
chunks (one push 0) and in escaped pane history (0 on plain rows). Every size in §5.1 is identical at `main`: this task
neither widens nor narrows them. Carried, by slug, to the follow-up masking task with the others (§11).
