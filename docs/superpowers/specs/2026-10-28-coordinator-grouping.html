Coordinator grouping: a programme reads as one unit

Three ways to tie child workspaces to their coordinator by something stronger than indentation. Each is shown in its three states. Click a header (or "Show workers") to collapse or expand it. Names and figures are examples.

  1. Collapsed by default. You see one line per programme: name, progress, time since it last moved, and a summary of its workers.
  2. A child that needs you breaks through. Even while collapsed, any child with a question, a permission or a failure is shown inside its programme. It is never hidden behind a click.
  3. Expanded shows everyone. Your choice is remembered per programme on this device.
  4. On the Status tab only rules 1 and 2 apply: there is no expand, so you see coordinators and the children that need you, nothing else.

AThe programme is a box

Coordinator and children share one bordered container. The coordinator is the box's header, and the children live in a recessed tray inside it. Membership is shown by the box, not by indentation. A stalled programme's whole border turns red.

BA colour rail runs down the group

Each programme gets its own colour. A thick rail in that colour runs down the left of the coordinator and every child, so where the colour stops, the programme stops. Children are lighter, smaller rows with no card of their own, so they read as parts of the coordinator rather than peers.

CA group header with a table under it

Like a grouped spreadsheet. A heavy rule and a large header row start each programme, with a count badge saying how many children it holds. The children are compact table rows under the header, sharing its columns. The next heavy rule is unmistakably the next programme.

These can combine: for example A's box with B's colour on the box's border. A first-time viewer should be able to answer "which workers belong to programme-c?" without reading a single indent.

A child workspace never appears as a top-level row anywhere. If its coordinator is gone, it shows inside a box titled "orphaned workers" so it is still visibly grouped.
