# Code of conduct

## The standard

This project adopts the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1][cc] as its code of conduct.
Rather than restate it here, where a paraphrase would drift from the text people actually
rely on, the linked document is the standard in full.

[cc]: https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/

The short version, in this project's own terms: engage with the work. Critique of a design,
a diff, or a measurement is the point of the place and can be as blunt as it needs to be.
Critique of a person is not, and neither is treating someone's inexperience, first language,
or unfamiliarity with this codebase's conventions as a defect in them.

## What that means concretely here

Two things about this repository make a specific difference:

**Disagreement is settled by measurement.** The codebase's own doctrine is that a comment
is a request and a red suite is a mechanism. Bring the measurement, not the volume — and
when someone shows you a measurement that contradicts you, that is the argument ending, not
an attack. Reviews here quote line numbers and counts; that is rigour, not hostility.

**Most contributors will be running an AI coding agent.** ccrc exists to drive fleets of
them. Work produced with an agent's help is welcome and needs no disclaimer. What is not
welcome is unread output: opening a pull request whose diff you cannot explain, or whose
tests you have not run, moves your review burden onto a single maintainer. Read what you
send. That is a conduct expectation as much as a quality one, because the cost lands on a
person.

## Reporting

This is a single-maintainer project, and honesty about the channel matters more than a
reassuring one that does not exist:

- **Conduct by a contributor, in public** — open an issue, or comment on the thread. Most
  of what needs saying can be said in the open.
- **Anything you would rather not raise publicly, or that concerns the maintainer** —
  contact the maintainer through their GitHub profile. There is no separate moderation
  team; a report goes to one person, and that person is a party to some of what might be
  reported. If that makes a channel you cannot use, GitHub's own
  [abuse reporting](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-content-spam)
  is independent of this project and is the right route.
- **A security vulnerability is not a conduct matter** — see `SECURITY.md`, which has a
  private channel of its own.

"Within a few days" is the truthful expectation for a response, the same as `SECURITY.md`
states. Enforcement, where it is needed, means editing or removing comments, closing pull
requests, and blocking accounts from the repository.
